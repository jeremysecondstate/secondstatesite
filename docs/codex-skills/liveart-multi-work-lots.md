# Lots containing multiple works

Use this reference when the queued lot contains a pair, group, suite, portfolio, illustrated book or other set of works. Keep **one queue item, one purchase and one Bid Board per auction lot**. Research components within that lot; do not create fictitious auction lots or split its bid. Apply the main guide's evidence rules, profit targets and bidding method, with the resale-override exception below.

## Establish what is included

Compare the original description with all available listing images. Record the stated quantity, visually confirmed quantity and any discrepancy. Several photographs may show the same work, details or its reverse; several compositions may be on one physical sheet. A book or intact portfolio is not automatically a collection of separately saleable prints. When quantity is unresolved, do not assume the largest count or multiply a value by it.

Assign stable component IDs within the lot, such as A, B, C and D, located by image number and position (for example, listing image 1, upper left). For repeated impressions, distinguish physical copies. Capture a compact inventory:

| Component / image position | Identity and confidence | Physical and edition details | Condition / eligibility | Sources and valuation basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A / image 1, upper left | Verified title, provisional match, or descriptive label | Medium; sheet/image size; edition, plate or catalogue reference if supported; hand-signed, printed signature, unsigned or unknown | Component-specific findings and exclusion, if any | Listing and identification URLs; mapped comp IDs/URLs; credited value or unresolved |

Keep listing claims separate from independently supported findings. Do not apply one sheet's signature, dimensions, edition or condition to every sheet unless the source explicitly covers them all. Apply the main guide's reproduction/unsigned exclusions to the intended resale units. A verified signature on a colophon or justification page may support an intact book/suite even when its interior plates are unsigned; it does not make loose sheets individually hand-signed. An excluded component receives no credited resale value under the default workflow, but its handling and disposal costs still matter.

## Identify works without inventing titles

Use labels such as **Unidentified composition A — red disk and black figure**, not an invented artwork title. Use **Untitled** as a formal title only when supported by a source. A suite or book title is context, not proof that each sheet has that title or belongs to that edition. Track identity as **verified**, **provisional** or **unresolved**, with the supporting source and remaining uncertainty.

Inspect individual listing images at useful resolution, including margins, signatures, numbering, inscriptions, publisher marks and versos when available. Search by artist plus the strongest available identifiers: portfolio/book, publisher, date, medium, dimensions, plate/catalogue number, inscriptions and visual composition. Search verified title variants when available; use broader artist/series searches when titles are absent. Compare candidate images from LiveArt, original auction records and credible publisher, museum or catalogue references. Use available visual search when it helps. Never treat a visual resemblance or search snippet alone as a verified identity.

Distinguish an original print, a book plate, a later reprint and an after-artist reproduction, as well as hand signatures versus signatures printed in the image. A design match does not establish edition, printing or signature type. Catalogue numbers, dates and titles must come from evidence. After a focused identification effort, keep unresolved fields explicit and continue with the components that can be researched. Do not repeatedly spend paid queries trying to force an unknown title into a match.

For a listing such as **4PC Joan Miro "A Toute Epreuve"**, start with four provisional component labels only if the listing supports four physical works. Check each sheet against the claimed publication and edition; the heading and photograph alone do not identify the plates or establish hand signatures. This is an identification example, not a conclusion about any queued lot.

## Match evidence to its actual unit of sale

Map every selected sale to component IDs or to the **whole group**. Record whether its hammer purchased one sheet, several works, an intact book or a complete portfolio. Keep the sale's real title, quantity, signature/edition facts and price unchanged. Never divide a group hammer into invented individual sales, or enter an estimated component value as a sold comp.

Prefer the same image and edition for individual works; for group comps also match composition, quantity, completeness and condition. A complete signed suite is not interchangeable with loose or incomplete sheets. If an exact title remains unknown, a clearly labeled analogue may support a value only when the subject's relevant medium, edition/product class, signature status, size and condition are sufficiently established. Explain material differences. Generic artist-level prices cannot resolve unknown product identity.

Use at most 25 deduplicated selected sales across the parent lot. One historical sale remains one evidence record even if it informs multiple components; explain any justified reuse for equivalent impressions. A different number of comps per component must not weight that component more heavily in the lot total. Keep alternative-strategy and identification-only sources in the research note rather than mixing them into the selected valuation basket.

## Choose a defensible resale basis

State whether the plan is resale **as a group**, **as separate works**, or a **known-component floor**. Do not sum separate-sale values for an intact collectible book/portfolio without supporting the proposed separation and any loss of completeness value. Compare group and separate resale where evidence supports both; base the Board on one practical supported plan, not a sum of both strategies.

- **Whole-group comps:** When selected prices are all comparable complete groups matching the subject lot, use the normal Working median with blank resale overrides. Explain differences; do not simply scale a suite's price by sheet count.
- **Component-based totals:** Establish Conservative, Working and Optimistic gross USD resale-hammer assumptions for each credited, independently saleable component. Working starts from that component's eligible comp median, with any supported adjustment explained; bound the other cases with evidence or explicitly stated supported assumptions. Sum the component values separately for each case. Use the existing Board's **three resale overrides** for these whole-lot totals. This is a specific exception to the normal blank-override rule. Fill all three so no case falls back to a pooled single-work median. A scenario total is an assumption derived from cited sales, never a new comp. Do not multiply one attractive print's value by the number of pieces unless equivalence of every credited copy is established.
- **Partially identified lots:** Assign no credited value where product identity or valuation remains unresolved, or a component is excluded; this does not claim the pieces are worthless. A missing formal title alone does not preclude a supported analogue valuation under the criteria above. A known-component floor is usable only when the supported components are independently saleable and the full purchase, handling and resale costs are accounted for. Label the result **partial valuation / known-component floor**, name the unvalued pieces and carry zero credited value for them across all three cases. Do not invent upside for unidentified extras. If identity, quantity, separability or costs prevent a defensible total, use **valuation unavailable**, zero selected sales and blank overrides as in the main guide; preserve identification findings and source links in the note.

The purchase hammer, buyer's premium, purchase tax and inbound shipping concern the **whole lot**. Apply the provisional USD 300 international shipping once per lot unless a quote or the actual shipment arrangement supports another amount. Account for restoration and handling of all relevant pieces. For separate resales, total the per-work fixed selling costs and seller-paid packing/shipping; reflect applicable commissions without charging any cost twice. Overrides are gross resale hammer, not net proceeds. The Board's acquisition costs and selling costs are aggregate inputs, with one selling-commission percentage. If different resale channels require different rates, document a conservative supported aggregate treatment; if the inputs cannot represent a defensible cost model, report the limitation instead of saving a misleading bid.

The USD 100 profit target, 10% return target, chosen purchase bid and ceiling apply to the whole lot. Preserve deliberate user choices when valid for the documented lot-level basis.

## Save an intelligible result in the existing UI

The application currently has no component rows or component-aware median calculation. Persist the inventory and math in the lot's **Research note** on the LiveArt Queue, within its 10,000-character limit. Preserve Jeremy's instructions verbatim and append or update a clearly labeled **Multi-work research** section. Keep evidence provenance and conclusions distinct from instructions; do not silently truncate either. Include:

- Count, component IDs/image positions, identity confidence and unresolved facts.
- Identification sources and the mapping from selected sale IDs/URLs to components or whole group.
- Resale plan; credited component values and Conservative/Working/Optimistic lot totals; excluded or unvalued pieces; aggregate cost assumptions.
- Whether the result is a whole-group valuation, component total, known-component floor or unavailable, and why.

For manually entered sales, the **Source evidence** field can also explain the component mapping and original sale quantity (keep within the UI's 3,000-character limit). This annotation does not replace faithful source transcription. Do not invent component fields or write around the UI to manufacture unsupported records. If sufficient valuation rationale cannot fit in the available note while preserving user instructions, report that persistence limitation rather than silently saving unexplained overrides.

Manual comp entry also checks the parent lot's artist. In a mixed-artist group, never relabel a component or sale to pass that check. Keep unsupported components and their sources in the note, report the evidence-entry limitation, and use a supported known-component floor only if its conditions above are met; otherwise leave valuation unavailable.

Save the note and selected evidence, then save the single Bid Board with the applicable whole-lot assumptions. Reload to check the saved note, selection and all three overrides (or their absence). The research panel may still show a pooled median for component comps; it is **not** the lot valuation. The documented totals and saved Board overrides must agree. If the UI cannot retain the necessary evidence, cost inputs or overrides, do not present its raw median as a valid multi-work Board.

The Research note is separate from the Board and is not included automatically in its email snapshot. In the lot's **Resale research**, identify the resale unit for each house and explain the values of the components it would offer. Use the same stable component IDs in seasonal proposal entries. Each separately proposed piece needs its own image and estimate. For an intact group, use one group entry and its aggregate estimate. In the final report, give the work count, identified versus unresolved components, valuation basis, whole-lot selected bid and MAX BID, and links to the saved Board, report, relevant proposals and queue note. Flag a known-component floor clearly.
